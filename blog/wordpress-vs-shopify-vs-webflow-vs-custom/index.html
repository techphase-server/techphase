WordPress vs Shopify vs Webflow vs Custom Code: Which Is Best for a Small Business?

5 November 2026 · Tech Phase

Short answer: Choose Shopify if selling products online is the main job of your website. Choose WordPress if you want the widest choice of plugins, hosts and developers, and full control of your content. Choose Webflow if design quality and easy visual editing matter most for a marketing site. Choose custom code only when your site needs something these platforms can't do well, such as a custom app, portal or unusual integration.

All four can produce a fast, search-friendly small business website. The right choice depends less on which is "best" and more on what your site has to do, who will edit it, and what you're willing to pay each month.

How do the four options compare at a glance?

WordPress Shopify Webflow Custom code
Best for Content-heavy sites, blogs, service businesses Online stores of any size Design-led marketing sites Apps, portals, unique features

Pricing changes often, so check the official pages before you decide: WordPress.com pricing, Shopify pricing and Webflow pricing. For a rough market view, Forbes Advisor lists Shopify at $39 to $2,300 a month plus transaction fees, and WordPress itself as free, with hosting and premium add-ons extra (Forbes Advisor).

When is WordPress the right choice?

WordPress is free, open-source software, so you can run it on almost any host and hire from a large pool of developers. It suits you if:

  • you publish regularly (blog posts, guides, news, case studies)
  • you want to own your content and move hosts freely
  • you need features that plugins already provide, such as booking, forms, memberships or multiple languages
  • you'll sell a modest product range with WooCommerce

The trade-off is maintenance. WordPress core, themes and plugins need regular updates, and every extra plugin adds weight and attack surface. A well-built WordPress site uses few plugins, sits on decent hosting, and has a backup and update routine from day one.

When is Shopify the right choice?

Shopify is built for selling. Checkout, payments, inventory, shipping, taxes and order management come built in, and hosting and security are handled for you. It suits you if:

  • products are the point of the website
  • you want a reliable checkout without managing servers
  • you sell in person as well as online, or across several channels

The trade-off is cost creep. The plan is only the start: apps, themes and transaction or payment fees add up, so total them before you commit. Shopify is also a poor fit for content-heavy sites with no store.

When is Webflow the right choice?

Webflow is a hosted visual builder with a built-in CMS. Designers can build precise layouts without plugins, and marketers can edit pages and CMS content visually. It suits you if:

  • the site is mainly for marketing, such as a service business, startup or portfolio
  • design and brand presentation matter a lot
  • your team wants to edit pages without touching code or managing updates

The trade-off is the platform. Hosting is tied to Webflow, plans have limits on CMS items, pages and features, and complex stores or custom logic can outgrow it. Webflow publishes extensive free training at Webflow University, which helps if your team will run the site.

When is custom code worth it?

Custom code means the site is written for you, for example a static site, a JavaScript framework, or a custom backend. It's worth it when:

  • your site is really an application, such as a customer portal, a quoting tool or a dashboard
  • you need integrations or performance that platforms can't reach
  • you want minimal running costs and no plugin or app fees, and you're happy to pay a developer for changes

The trade-off is dependence. If only one person understands the code, you're stuck. Insist on owning the repository, getting basic documentation, and a handover that another developer could pick up.

Which platform is best for SEO?

All four can rank well. Google's own SEO starter guide focuses on useful content, clear page titles and structure, descriptive URLs and a site Google can crawl. Any of these platforms supports that. SEO results come mostly from content and execution, not the logo on the platform. What to check on any platform:

  • editable page titles, meta descriptions and URLs
  • clean heading structure and fast pages on mobile (test with PageSpeed Insights)
  • an automatic XML sitemap and a way to add 301 redirects
  • structured data, either built in, via a plugin, or added by hand

If you're switching platforms, the redirects matter more than the platform. Our website redesign SEO checklist explains how to keep your rankings.

How do you decide in five minutes?

Answer these in order:

  1. Is selling products the main goal? Shopify, or WooCommerce if you also publish a lot of content.
  2. Will you publish content every week? WordPress.
  3. Is it a marketing site where design matters most, edited by non-developers? Webflow.
  4. Does it need app-like features or custom integrations? Custom code, or a platform plus custom pieces.
  5. Still unsure? Pick the option your team can edit confidently, with the lowest total cost over three years.

What else do people ask about choosing a platform?

Is WordPress or Shopify better for a small business?

Shopify is usually better if your main goal is selling products online, because checkout, payments and hosting are built in. WordPress is usually better for content-led service businesses that want full control and the widest choice of plugins and hosts.

Is Webflow better than WordPress?

Webflow is often easier for design-led marketing sites edited by non-developers, because hosting and updates are handled for you. WordPress gives more flexibility, more plugins and freedom to move hosts, but it needs regular updates.

Is a custom-coded website better for SEO?

Not automatically. All four options can rank well. Good content, clear titles and headings, fast mobile pages and correct redirects matter far more than the platform.

Can I switch platforms later?

Yes, but plan it as a migration. Export your content, map every old URL to a new one with 301 redirects, and check Search Console after launch so you don't lose rankings.

Which platform has the lowest ongoing cost?

It depends on your setup. A simple custom or WordPress site on modest hosting can be cheap to run, while Shopify and Webflow charge monthly plans that include hosting. Add up plans, apps, plugins and fees over three years before you decide.
