Custom Website Design for Small Business: A Practical Guide

31 October 2026 · Tech Phase

For a small business, a website has one job: turning visitors into calls, bookings and sales. Looking good is part of that, but it isn't the goal.

This guide covers what custom website design for a small business should include, how to choose a platform, what affects the cost, how to redesign without losing your Google rankings, and what to ask a web design agency before you hire one.

Custom design vs a template: what's the difference?

A template gives you someone else's layout, and you fit your business into it. Custom design starts the other way round. It begins with your customers, your offer and the one action you want visitors to take: call, book, buy or enquire. The pages are then designed around that action.

Templates can be fine for a very simple site. Custom design is worth it when:

  • you compete with other local or online businesses for the same customers
  • your service needs explaining before people will contact you
  • your current site gets visits but few enquiries

What a small-business website should include

Whatever platform you choose, these are the foundations to insist on.

A clear path to one action

Every key page should make the next step obvious, with a visible phone number, booking button or quote form. If visitors have to hunt for how to contact you, many won't.

Mobile-first design

Many small-business visitors arrive on their phones, and Google mainly uses the mobile version of a site for indexing and ranking. See Google's guide to mobile-first indexing. Design for the phone first, then scale up.

Speed

Slow pages frustrate visitors and can hold back your search performance. Lean code and optimised images help. Google's Core Web Vitals explain the loading, responsiveness and visual-stability measures it looks at.

SEO foundations at launch

At launch, a new site should already have:

  • clean URLs
  • one clear main heading per page
  • unique page titles and meta descriptions
  • schema markup
  • an XML sitemap
  • Google Search Console set up

Google's own SEO Starter Guide is a good plain-English reference.

Accessibility

A site that works with screen readers and keyboards is better for every visitor. The W3C's WCAG guidelines are the standard reference, and the US Department of Justice has guidance on web accessibility for businesses.

Easy updates and clear ownership

You should be able to edit text, images and blog posts yourself. Your domain, hosting, code and design files should be in your name once the work is paid for.

Choosing a platform: WordPress, Shopify, Webflow or custom code

There's no single best platform. The right one depends on what the site needs to do.

  • WordPress is flexible and easy to edit, with custom themes or block-based builds, and WooCommerce if you need a store.
  • Shopify is built for ecommerce, from theme customisation and full store builds to migrations and app integrations.
  • Webflow suits marketing sites that need design control and easy editing without plugins.
  • Custom code is for web apps, SaaS marketing sites and anything off-the-shelf tools can't handle.

A good web design agency for small businesses will recommend one and explain why. If they only ever build on one platform, every project will be pushed onto it.

How much does a website cost for a small business?

Honestly, it depends, and anyone quoting a price before they understand your project is guessing. The main cost drivers are:

  • Page count: a focused landing page is a very different job from a multi-page site.
  • Features: booking, ecommerce, memberships, integrations and multiple languages all add work.
  • Platform: a WordPress brochure site, a Shopify store and a custom web app need different skills.
  • Content: whether your text and images are ready, or need writing and sourcing.
  • Aftercare: whether you want monthly updates, backups and small changes after launch.

The way to get a reliable number is a written scope: a sitemap, page list, features and timeline, with a fixed quote attached. If you compare agencies, compare their scopes, not just their headline prices.

Redesigning without losing your Google rankings

If your current site is slow, dated or not producing enquiries, a redesign can help more than starting from zero. The biggest risk is losing rankings you've already earned.

The standard way to protect them:

  1. Review the existing site first: speed, structure, content, SEO and how visitors currently reach your contact page.
  2. Keep the content that already ranks, and improve it rather than deleting it.
  3. Map every old URL to its new URL with a 301 redirect, page by page. Google explains this in its guide to site moves with URL changes.
  4. Check Search Console after launch for crawl errors and pages that dropped out.

Questions to ask a web design agency before you hire

  1. Will I see designs before any code is written?
  2. Will there be a staging link I can check during the build?
  3. What exactly is in the written scope, and what counts as a change?
  4. Are SEO foundations and Search Console setup included at launch?
  5. Who owns the domain, hosting, code and design files when the project ends?
  6. How are payments structured? Milestones tied to delivered work are a good sign on larger projects.
  7. How and when will we communicate, and how quickly do you reply?
